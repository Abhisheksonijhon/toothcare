Dental care after 50: dry mouth, worn teeth and dentures

Teeth can last a lifetime. These are the changes that matter after 50 and how to look after them.

Written forSeniorsAdults

Key points

  • Dry mouth raises the risk of cavities, so sip water often
  • Exposed roots decay faster, so fluoride toothpaste matters
  • Clean dentures daily and remove them at night
  • Report any mouth ulcer lasting more than two weeks

With good care, natural teeth can last a lifetime. After 50, a few changes deserve extra attention.

Dry mouth

Many common medicines reduce saliva. Saliva washes away food and neutralises acid, so a dry mouth means more cavities. Sip water through the day, chew sugar-free gum, and tell your dentist about any medicines you take.

Exposed roots

As gums recede, root surfaces become exposed. Roots have no hard enamel and decay faster, so use a fluoride toothpaste, brush gently and clean between the teeth daily.

Gums and general health

Gum disease is more common with age, and it is linked with conditions such as diabetes. Regular check-ups and cleaning help keep gums firm and teeth secure.

If you wear dentures

  • Clean them every day with a soft brush and denture cleaner, not toothpaste
  • Take them out at night and keep them in water
  • Brush your gums and tongue too
  • See your dentist if they feel loose, rub, or cause sore spots

Loose dentures can sometimes be steadied with dental implants, which give them something firm to hold on to.

Book a check-up at least once a year. Any mouth ulcer or patch that does not heal within two weeks should be examined by a dentist.
